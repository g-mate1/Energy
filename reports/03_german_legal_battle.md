# The German Legal Battle over the Network Equity Return and the Randl/Zechner Expert Reports

*Compiled from public sources as of June 2026. This document is for information only, is not legal advice, and reflects an evolving body of regulatory and judicial material. Several primary documents (the Bundesnetzagentur press releases, the OLG Düsseldorf and BGH decisions) were available only via search-engine summaries at the time of writing because the underlying servers blocked direct retrieval; figures, dates and case numbers should be re-verified against the primary court and regulatory documents before being relied upon.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

Between 2021 and 2026 Germany fought a high-stakes legal battle over the allowed return on equity for regulated electricity and gas networks. The Bundesnetzagentur (BNetzA), relying heavily on a scientific expert report (*Gutachten*) by Professors **Otto Randl** and **Josef Zechner** of WU Vienna together with **Frontier Economics**, cut the pre-tax equity return for new network assets to **5.07 %** and for existing assets to **3.51 %** for the fourth regulatory period — well over 25 % below the third-period figures. Network operators, supported by the industry associations BDEW and VKU, challenged the determination before the Oberlandesgericht (OLG) Düsseldorf. In August 2023 the OLG annulled the determination and ordered redetermination, accepting much of the operators' methodological critique. The Bundesgerichtshof (BGH), however, reversed the OLG across a long series of decisions in 2024 and 2025, confirming the BNetzA's figures and setting a deferential standard of judicial review. The dispute unfolded against the backdrop of the European Court of Justice's (ECJ) ruling in **C-718/18** (2 September 2021), which stripped the German federal government of detailed rule-making power and converted the equity-return exercise into a discretionary BNetzA *Festlegung*.

## 2. The BNetzA determinations

### 2.1 The 12 October 2021 *Festlegung*

On **12 October 2021** the BNetzA's Beschlusskammer 4 issued two parallel determinations setting the calculatory equity return (*kalkulatorischer Eigenkapitalzinssatz* / *Wagniszuschlag*) for the fourth regulatory period [1][2][8]:

- **BK4-21-055** — electricity network operators, under **§ 7(6) StromNEV**;
- **BK4-21-056** — gas network operators, under **§ 7(6) GasNEV** [8].

The figures (each pre-(corporate-)tax) were uniform across electricity and gas [2][8]:

| Asset class | 4th period (Oct 2021) | 3rd period (for contrast) |
|---|---|---|
| **New assets** (*Neuanlagen*) | **5.07 %** (≈ 5.8 % incl. trade tax) | **6.91 %** |
| **Existing assets** (*Bestands-/Altanlagen*) | **3.51 %** | **5.12 %** |

The fourth regulatory period runs **2023–2027 for gas** and **2024–2028 for electricity** [2][8]. The third-period figures themselves represented a sharp cut from the second period (new-asset rate of **9.05 %**), so the trajectory had been steeply downward for over a decade [1]. The new-asset rate of 5.07 % was widely treated as the headline figure and the binding floor for revenue-cap calculations [3].

### 2.2 Methodology and legal basis

The legal architecture combined the EnWG, the incentive-regulation ordinance (**ARegV**) and **§ 7 StromNEV/GasNEV**. Under § 7(4) StromNEV/GasNEV the equity return is built from a **risk-free base rate** plus a **risk premium** (*Wagniszuschlag*) for network-operator-specific business risk [3][4]:

- **Base rate (Basiszins):** historically the **ten-year trailing average of the *Umlaufsrendite*** (yield on outstanding domestic public bonds). For the fourth period this average had fallen to **0.74 %**, down **1.75 percentage points** from **2.49 %** in the third period — a direct consequence of the long low-rate environment [3][9].
- **Risk premium:** derived via the **Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)** — a market risk premium (*Marktrisikoprämie*) multiplied by a network beta — and set at roughly **3 %** (the BNetzA increased it by about **0.395 percentage points** versus its July 2021 draft, which, with tax effects, lifted the new-asset rate from the draft 4.59 % to 5.07 %) [1].
- A **tax factor of ~1.226** grossed the figure up for corporate income tax [4].

### 2.3 Why the cut was contentious

The reduction was deeply contentious because it arrived as inflation and market interest rates were turning sharply **upward** in 2022–2023. Operators and their associations argued that anchoring the base rate to a ten-year trailing average mechanically embedded stale, ultra-low yields and could not capture the rate reversal, just as Germany needed to mobilise **hundreds of billions of euros** of grid investment for the energy transition [3][14]. BDEW's Kerstin Andreae and VKU's Ingbert Liebing called the cut "a completely wrong signal" that endangered operators' investment capacity [14]. Consumer advocates (vzbv) took the opposite view, arguing the rates were still generous [13].

### 2.4 The January 2024 partial uplift for new investment

Responding both to the rate environment and to the OLG's August 2023 ruling (below), the BNetzA published a fresh determination on **24 January 2024** introducing a **time-variable** equity return for **new investments made from 1 January 2024** within the capital-cost surcharge (*Kapitalkostenaufschlag*). On then-current forecasts this implied roughly **7.09 %** (≈ 8.1 % including trade tax) for 2024, replacing the ten-year average with a base rate keyed to the most recent completed quarter, while leaving the **existing-asset rate unchanged at 5.07 %** (incl. trade tax) [10][11][12]. This created a three-tier landscape: legacy assets, fourth-period assets, and post-2024 new investments.

## 3. The Randl/Zechner *Gutachten* and the methodological attack

The determination rested on the **"Wissenschaftliches Gutachten zur Ermittlung der Zuschläge für unternehmerische Wagnisse von Strom- und Gasnetzbetreibern"** (July 2021) by **Prof. Josef Zechner**, **Prof. Otto Randl** and **Frontier Economics** [5][6]. Its most consequential recommendation was to estimate the market risk premium from a single long-run **historical** source — the **Dimson, Marsh & Staunton (DMS)** dataset as published in the **Credit Suisse Global Investment Returns Yearbook 2021** (up to 90 countries, 1900–2020) — reflecting the increasing integration of euro-area capital markets [5][8].

Operators attacked the methodology on several fronts [7][9]:

- **Market risk premium / total market return:** reliance on DMS historical averages alone, without cross-checking against forward-looking or implied-cost-of-equity methods, and the alleged non-replicability of the result and non-disclosure of the country-weighting scheme.
- **Base rate / *Umlaufsrendite* window:** the ten-year averaging window suppressed the rate rebound and understated the cost of equity in an inflationary environment.
- **Time-variability:** critics noted that Randl himself (and the earlier Stehle/Betzer work) had acknowledged the market risk premium varies over time, undercutting a static historical estimate.
- **Beta and peer-group selection:** the choice and adjustment of the comparator beta.
- **Treatment of inflation and international comparison:** the BNetzA's "restrictive and singular" reliance on DMS sat at odds with peer regulators, none of which (on the operators' evidence) fixed the premium on DMS data alone.

For the **fifth** regulatory period the BNetzA again engaged the **Frontier / Zechner / Randl** consortium for a methodology *Gutachten* (2025), alongside a counter-report by **NERA** commissioned by BDEW [5][6].

## 4. The OLG Düsseldorf proceedings (first instance)

Roughly **900 network operators** filed appeals (*Beschwerden*) with the **3. Kartellsenat** of the **OLG Düsseldorf**, which bundled them into **14 representative test cases** (*Musterverfahren*) [7][15][16]. By **decision of 30 August 2023** the senate **annulled** the BNetzA's determinations (BK4-21-055 / -056) and ordered redetermination consistent with the court's legal view [15][16].

**Case numbers (Aktenzeichen):** the proceedings carry references in the form **3 Kart …/21**. The lead/representative case is **3 Kart 311/21**; further published decisions of the same date include **3 Kart 498/21, 544/21, 689/21, 757/21, 775/21 and 878/21** [7][15]. (The senate's older decisions used the prefix "VI-3 Kart"; the 2023 decisions appear in the NRWE database as "3 Kart".)

**What the court held.** The senate found the determinations unlawful primarily because the BNetzA had derived the market risk premium **solely from the DMS historical data series without subjecting it to any further verification — at minimum a supplementary plausibility check** [7][15]. The court reasoned that [7]:

- the BNetzA's "restrictive and singular" reliance on DMS was itself a reason to test reliability, since **none of the comparator regulators** examined fixed the premium on DMS data alone;
- a **17-country comparison** produced an average after-tax cost of equity of about **6.05 %** (range **4.30 %–7.50 %**), and an expert estimate of the **implicit/realised cost of equity** of comparable firms of **6.82 %–7.04 %** — both materially above the BNetzA's result, indicating a lack of plausibility.

Notably, the court accepted that the BNetzA's **written reasons satisfied the formal requirements of § 73(1) EnWG** (it had explained why it used long-run DMS data and addressed criticism of replicability and the undisclosed weighting scheme) — the defect was **substantive**, not a failure to give reasons [7]. The senate **admitted the *Rechtsbeschwerde* (appeal on points of law) to the BGH** [16]. The electricity and gas proceedings ran in parallel on materially identical reasoning.

## 5. The BGH dimension and the EU-law backdrop

### 5.1 ECJ C-718/18 (2 September 2021) — the regulator's expanded discretion

On **2 September 2021** the ECJ (Grand Chamber) ruled in **Commission v Germany, C-718/18** that Germany had breached the 2009 Electricity and Gas Directives by allowing the **federal government** — via detailed ordinances issued under **§ 24 EnWG** (the **StromNEV, GasNEV, StromNZV and ARegV**) — to prescribe the methods and parameters for network tariffs, a competence the directives reserve **exclusively to the independent national regulatory authority** [17][18][19]. Germany was required to remove this political/legislative pre-determination. Through the **EnWG amendment of November 2023** the legislature abolished the federal ordinance-making power for network access/charges and replaced it with a **determination (Festlegung) competence for the BNetzA** [17][19].

The consequence for the equity-return litigation was pivotal: the exercise migrated from the application of binding ordinance parameters to a **discretionary regulatory *Festlegung*** within the BNetzA's own **margin of appreciation (Beurteilungsspielraum / Regulierungsermessen)** — which in turn raised the question of how intensively courts may review the BNetzA's **choice of method**.

### 5.2 The BGH reverses the OLG

The BGH reversed the OLG across a series of *Rechtsbeschwerde* decisions:

- **17 December 2024** — lead **electricity** decisions, including **EnVR 79/23** (and **EnVR 85/23, 88/23, 94/23**), holding that the BNetzA had set the equity return for electricity distribution networks **without legal error** [9][20][21].
- **25 February 2025** — lead **gas** decisions, including **EnVR 83/23, 86/23, 90/23 and 93/23**, rejecting the appeals of (per reporting) originally **937 gas network operators** and again reversing the OLG's 30 August 2023 ruling, with costs on the operators [2][22].
- **9 September 2025** — further confirmatory decisions such as **EnVR 82/23**, extending the result to remaining proceedings [16][23].

**The legal standard.** The BGH's guiding principle (*Leitsatz*) sets a markedly **deferential** bar: the regulator's **selection decision** among recognised scientific methods can be set aside **only** if the chosen approach is *"grundlegend ungeeignet"* (fundamentally unsuitable) to fulfil its function within the model, **or** if **another approach is "deutlich überlegen" (clearly superior)** once all relevant factors — suitability, data availability, the effort to establish it, and the precision and reliability of results — are weighed [22][24]. On that standard, the BGH held the OLG had **wrongly** treated the BNetzA's DMS-based approach as defective: indicators that other methods exist, or that a plausibility cross-check is conceivable, do **not** compel one, since requiring such a check would always force a methodologically pluralistic approach [22][24]. The court accepted that the **risk-premium uplift** the BNetzA had made — to offset the risk that the fallen base rate (0.74 % vs 2.49 %) understated the surcharge — was **plausible and adequately reasoned** [9].

The BGH expressly linked its deference to the BNetzA's **post-C-718/18 status**: at the December 2024 hearing the presiding judge of the Kartellsenat (Wolfgang Kirchhoff) signalled that the agency enjoys **discretion in choosing the appropriate calculation method**, which courts should not displace — a position tied to the strengthening of the BNetzA's independence and competences in the November 2023 EnWG reform [20]. (Earlier reporting around the BGH's first hearing on 17 December 2024 had suggested the court might *tighten* plausibility requirements; the reasoned decisions ultimately came out **the other way**, in the BNetzA's favour [22].)

## 6. Current status (mid-2026), stakes and outlook

### 6.1 Status

As of mid-2026 the litigation over the **fourth-period** equity return is, in substance, **closed in the BNetzA's favour**. The OLG's annulment was reversed; the **5.07 % (new) / 3.51 % (existing)** figures stand, supplemented by the January 2024 time-variable uplift (~7.09 %) for post-2024 new investment [2][9][22]. Because the BGH **upheld** the original determination, the redetermination the OLG had ordered did **not** ultimately materialise as a forced re-do of the methodology.

### 6.2 Financial stakes

The economic significance is large. Equity returns flow directly into the revenue cap and thus into **network charges (Netzentgelte)** paid by households, businesses and industry [20]. Each tenth of a percentage point, applied to the multi-billion-euro regulated asset base across ~900 electricity and gas operators and a fourth period running to 2027/2028, translates into substantial sums. Operators argued the rates were too low to attract the equity needed for the *Energiewende* grid build-out; consumer and some analytical voices countered that headline regulatory rates understate actual returns — one BNE analysis cited a market-share-weighted commercial equity return of ~30 % for the 18 largest distribution operators in 2024, far above the regulatory 5.07 %/3.51 % [25] (a figure that mixes leverage and accounting effects and is contested).

### 6.3 Outlook — the fifth period and the new WACC

The most important forward-looking change is methodological. From the **fifth regulatory period — 2028 (gas) / 2029 (electricity)** — the BNetzA intends to move to a **standardised WACC** ("Weighted Average Cost of Capital") with a fixed notional capital structure of **40 % equity / 60 % debt**, a market-indexed debt-cost component, and a base rate tied to recent rather than decade-old yields [26][9]. On the BNetzA's own 2025 projections the implied **equity return could rise to roughly 6.5 %–7 %** for existing-asset investment — a substantial increase that reflects both higher market rates and methodological reform [26]. The agency launched method-determination consultations around **30 June 2025**, again drawing on a **Frontier/Zechner/Randl (2025)** methodology report and an independent plausibility *Kurzgutachten*, with BDEW commissioning a competing **NERA** analysis [5][6][26]. A further shortening of the regulatory cycle (to three years) is contemplated from the sixth period to track cost developments more quickly [26].

The episode's lasting doctrinal legacy is the **judicial-review standard**: after C-718/18 and the BGH's 2024–2025 jurisprudence, the BNetzA enjoys a **wide, only thinly reviewable margin** in choosing among recognised cost-of-capital methods. Future challenges will have to clear the high "fundamentally unsuitable / clearly superior" threshold rather than merely show that a different, plausible method would have produced a higher number.

## 8. Caveats on verification

- **The first BGH reversal date.** Sources consistently place the BGH's reversal in two principal tranches — **electricity on 17 December 2024** and **gas on 25 February 2025** — with additional confirmations on 9 September 2025. Some general-press summaries loosely describe "a February 2024" reversal; that appears to be a transposition of **2025**. Treat 17 Dec 2024 / 25 Feb 2025 as the best-supported dates, but confirm the exact grouping of EnVR case numbers against the BGH database.
- **Case-number completeness.** The OLG bundled 14 *Musterverfahren* and the BGH decided a large series of EnVR appeals (roughly the EnVR 79/23–94/23 range, plus EnVR 82/23). The specific numbers listed above are corroborated in source snippets, but the full roster and the precise lead case per sector should be checked against NRWE / the BGH.
- **Figures.** The 5.07 % (new) and 3.51 % (existing) pre-tax rates, the 6.91 %/5.12 % third-period figures, the 0.74 % base rate (down from 2.49 %), and the ~7.09 % January-2024 new-investment rate are each corroborated by multiple sources but should be confirmed against the primary BNetzA determinations. (Note: a parallel report in this set cites the existing-asset rate for gas as 3.03 %; the precise existing-asset split between electricity and gas should be confirmed against BK4-21-055/056.)
- **Operator-return statistics.** The "~30 % commercial equity return" figure is a contested third-party analysis and is not a regulatory parameter.
