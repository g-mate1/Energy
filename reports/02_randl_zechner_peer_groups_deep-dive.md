# Peer Groups in the Randl/Zechner Expert Reports for German Gas and Electricity Network Returns — Deep Dive

*This report was compiled from publicly available sources as of June 2026 and is a deep-dive expansion of the companion note "Peer Groups in the Randl/Zechner Expert Reports for German Gas and Electricity Network Returns." It is for information only and is not investment, legal or regulatory advice. The topic hinges on the exact membership of a small peer group, and the decisive primary documents — above all the Frontier/Zechner/Randl Gutachten hosted on bundesnetzagentur.de (BNetzA file BK4-21-0055 for electricity / BK4-21-0056 for gas), the FragDenStaat freedom-of-information disclosure, and the OLG Düsseldorf / BGH decisions — could not be retrieved verbatim in the course of this research because the relevant servers (bundesnetzagentur.de, e-control.at, bdew.de, fragdenstaat.de, frontier-economics.com, nrwe.justiz.nrw.de, openjur.de, bundesgerichtshof.de) systematically return HTTP 403 to automated requests and sit behind a host allow-list. The substantive findings below were triangulated from (a) search-engine indexing that quotes the full text of those same PDFs and court decisions, (b) official press releases and consultant statements, and (c) the authors' methodologically identical reports for the Austrian regulator E-Control and the German railway regulator. Where a fact rests only on this indirect channel — and especially where the exact membership of the narrowest "pure" peer set is concerned — it is flagged explicitly. **Readers must confirm the exact peer-group membership, the de-/re-levering inputs (notably the debt beta) and the precise beta and gearing figures against the primary Gutachten in BK4-21-0055/0056 before relying on them.***

---

## 0. Executive summary and what this deep-dive adds

The Bundesnetzagentur's (BNetzA) allowed equity return for the fourth regulatory period rested on a single number — a **risk factor (beta) of 0.81** — multiplied by a **market risk premium (MRP) of 3.70 %** and topped up by a discretionary uplift of about **0.395 percentage points** to produce a **Wagniszuschlag (risk surcharge) of ≈ 3.39 %**. That beta was estimated from a **comparison group of foreign, listed "pure" network operators** screened down from an extended universe of roughly **71 listed energy companies**. The peer group — its composition, its purity filter, and the beta machinery applied to it — was the single most contested technical input in the four-and-a-half-year legal battle that followed, and it is the subject of this report.

Relative to the base note, this deep-dive adds, among other things:

- **A verified reference market index.** The German energy Gutachten used the **FTSE All-World Index (and its sub-indices)** as the CAPM market portfolio — previously asserted, now corroborated from indexed text of the Gutachten.
- **A reconstructed four-stage screening funnel** — **~71 → ~23 (extended sample) → ~12 (narrower sample) → ~9–11 "pure" operators** — with the asset beta computed as the unweighted arithmetic mean of the risk factors of **eleven comparison companies** as of 31 December 2020.
- **A resolution of the 0.81 puzzle.** 0.81 is the **re-levered (levered) equity beta**; the underlying **asset (unlevered) beta lies in a 0.34–0.42 range** (central value ≈ 0.38), reconcilable with E-Control's ≈ 0.325 unlevered beta.
- **A correction on the debt beta.** The methodologically identical E-Control reports apply Modigliani–Miller de-/re-levering **without a debt beta (debt beta = 0)**; this casts doubt on the base note's "non-zero debt beta" assumption for the German report, which could not be confirmed.
- **A decode of "BIEGTRDT."** The string is **Bloomberg's index code for "BI Europe Gas Transmission & Distribution Top Competitive Peers,"** the core comparator group of the E-Control gas-transmission report — not a company-initials acronym. Its core membership (Enagás, Fluxys Belgium, Italgas, National Grid, REN, Snam) notably does **not** contain Terna or Elia, which sit in E-Control's *electricity* peer set; the base note's single combined "BIEGTRDT" table therefore conflated the gas and electricity comparator sets, and is corrected below.
- **External validation.** For its 2024–25 RIIO-T3 review, the UK regulator Ofgem independently expanded its beta-comparator pool by adding **Enagás, Redeia (Red Eléctrica), Italgas, Snam and Terna** — the same continental "pure network operator" universe — and its adviser Oxera judged their business mixes and regulatory regimes "sufficiently similar" to GB networks.
- **A fuller annotated literature review** of every primary Gutachten and counter-Gutachten, with exact titles, clients and dates, including the Stehle/Betzer MRP report, the 2025 fifth-period methodology report, and the fact that Oxera's critique provoked a direct rebuttal from Dimson, Marsh and Staunton themselves.

The honest bottom line is unchanged from the base note and reinforced by deeper digging: the **eight-company benchmark set from the freedom-of-information disclosure — E.ON, Elia, Enagás, National Grid, Red Eléctrica/Redeia, REN, Snam, Terna — remains the only firm attribution for the German report**, with E.ON named but screened out of the narrow asset-beta set. No additional German constituent could be firmly confirmed, and several names commonly assumed to be present (Italgas, Fluxys, A2A, SSE, EDP, RWE, MVV) remain unconfirmed for Germany — most belonging instead to the Austrian E-Control sets or excluded outright.

## 1. The authors and the full corpus of their relevant Gutachten

### 1.1 Otto Randl and Josef Zechner

**Josef Zechner** is Senior Professor of Finance and Investments at WU Vienna (Vienna University of Economics and Business), attached to the Institute for Finance, Banking and Insurance. He is one of the most decorated finance academics in the German-speaking world: speaker of the doctoral programme Vienna Graduate School of Finance (VGSF), a CEPR Research Fellow, a member of the management board of the Spängler IQAM Research Center (a joint research centre of Spängler IQAM Invest, TU Wien and WU Wien), a past president of the European Finance Association, the German Finance Association and the Western Finance Association, a former Managing Editor of the *Review of Finance* (2003–2012, with Marco Pagano), a full member of the Austrian Academy of Sciences and an honorary doctor of LMU Munich [1][21][22].

**Otto Randl** is Professor of Endowment Management at WU Vienna, also at the Institute for Finance, Banking and Insurance, and is (co-)head of WU's Research Institute for Capital Markets (ISK) and an associate faculty member of VGSF. He took his habilitation (Privatdozent) in December 2017 and has explicitly "worked as a free-lance consultant in projects for regulators and financial institutions" — the professional context for the body of regulatory work catalogued here [2][23]. (Whether Randl holds a *formal* role at the Spängler IQAM Research Center could not be confirmed; the centre's published management list names Dangl, Halling and Zechner, not Randl — treat any IQAM attribution for Randl as unverified.)

The two are, together with **Frontier Economics**, the standing academic experts on regulatory cost of capital for the **German Bundesnetzagentur** (energy networks and, with IGES Institut, railway infrastructure) and the **Austrian regulator E-Control** (gas and electricity transmission and distribution). BNetzA describes its risk-premium methodology as a continuous lineage running through the Frontier/(Zechner/Randl) reports "since 2008, 2011 and 2016," then 2021, then 2025 [9][24].

Two features of the corpus matter for the rest of this report. First, the **methodology is essentially identical across the 2016 and 2021 German energy reports, the E-Control reports and the railway reports**: a CAPM/WACC built on a broad global market portfolio, an MRP from the Dimson–Marsh–Staunton (DMS) long-run historical dataset, and a beta estimated from a comparison group of listed network operators, Vasicek-adjusted and Modigliani–Miller de-/re-levered. This is why the E-Control and railway reports — which are more explicit on the beta mechanics — are legitimate cross-checks for the German energy figures. Second, the **companion Stehle/Betzer report** on the market risk premium was a *separate* Gutachten by different authors (Richard Stehle, Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin; André Betzer, Bergische Universität Wuppertal), commissioned by BNetzA as part of the *same* package supporting the fourth-period decisions; it examined whether central-bank-implied MRP methods could be transferred to the regulatory MRP and broadly supported BNetzA's relatively low MRP stance [27].

The 2021 energy report underpinned the **Eigenkapitalzinssatz decisions BK4-21-0055 (electricity) and BK4-21-0056 (gas), both issued 12 October 2021** and published in the BNetzA Amtsblatt on 20 October 2021 [3][13]. Its framework was carried into the **24 January 2024 redetermination** for *new* installations (a time-variable rate = annually variable base rate + a constant 3 % risk surcharge), while BNetzA expressly held the 2021 methodology and the existing-installation rate to remain appropriate [4].

## 2. The peer group in depth

### 2.1 The firm attribution: the eight-company benchmark set

In the words used by the OLG Düsseldorf, BNetzA's beta was derived "anhand einer Vergleichsgruppe ausländischer börsennotierter reiner Netzbetreiber" — "on the basis of a comparison group of foreign, listed *pure* network operators" [11][12]. Consistent with the legislator's requirement of a uniform equity rate for both sectors, the Gutachten did **not** publish separate gas-only and electricity-only peer groups; a **single combined European energy-network peer group** fed one beta and one Wagniszuschlag [3][11].

The only firm, document-level attribution of the actual constituents is a **Bundesnetzagentur freedom-of-information disclosure (via FragDenStaat)** of the Frontier/Zechner/Randl Gutachten, which lists the international benchmarking/peer companies as: **E.ON, Elia (Elia System Operator), Enagás, National Grid, Red Eléctrica, Redes Energéticas Nacionais (REN), Snam and Terna** [13]. This set is internally coherent — it is precisely the population of large, listed European TSO/network operators — and is corroborated by NERA's international comparison for the BDEW [5][14]. It could not be re-opened verbatim in this research (the FragDenStaat page returns 403), so the *membership* is treated as firm (it matches the base note and multiple independent extractions) while the exact boundary of the disclosed list is, strictly, corroborated rather than re-verified.

**The wide-set / narrow-set distinction.** A point that reconciles an apparent contradiction: E.ON appears in the disclosed *benchmark* universe but is **excluded from the narrow "pure" asset-beta set**, because too large a share of its business is non-network (see §3.1). So "E.ON is named" and "E.ON is excluded" are both correct — they refer to the wide benchmark set and the narrow asset-beta set respectively. The pure asset-beta set is therefore the six continental TSOs (Elia, Enagás, Red Eléctrica, REN, Snam, Terna) plus National Grid, with E.ON removed — seven firmly-named firms, expanded to the **eleven companies** that actually entered the asset-beta average (see §2.3 and §4.6) once additional, less-firmly-attributed names are included.

### 2.2 Profiles of the firmly-attributed constituents

All eight are major listed European energy network operators. Regulated-revenue shares below are qualitative bands from secondary sources and company reports (the precise 2021 figures in the Gutachten could not be read); treat the percentages as indicative.

- **E.ON SE — Germany (XETRA: EOAN; ISIN DE000ENAG999; DAX).** Following the 2019–2020 Innogy/RWE asset swap, E.ON is predominantly an energy-networks and customer-solutions group — Europe's largest distribution-grid operator (~1.5 million km of lines across Germany, Sweden and Central/Eastern Europe) — but it retains a very large retail/supply business. Networks are the majority of *adjusted EBIT* (often cited at ~75–80 %) but a much smaller share of *revenue*, because the commodity-heavy supply business carries large, low-margin turnover. This revenue-versus-EBIT split is exactly why E.ON fails a *revenue-based* purity screen and was screened out of the asset-beta set. Currency: EUR. *In German benchmark set: yes (named), but excluded from the pure set.*
- **Elia Group — Belgium (Euronext Brussels: ELI; BEL 20).** A near-pure electricity transmission system operator: it runs the Belgian high-voltage grid and owns ~80 % of the German TSO **50Hertz** (one of Germany's four electricity TSOs, ~27 % of German transmission). Revenue is essentially all regulated tariff income. Its part-ownership of a large slice of the German grid makes it an unusually apposite comparator. Currency: EUR. *Firm.*
- **Enagás S.A. — Spain (Bolsa de Madrid: ENG; IBEX 35).** The Spanish gas transmission system operator and technical manager of the gas system (~12,000 km of high-pressure pipelines, plus regasification and storage), with some international stakes. Predominantly regulated gas infrastructure. Currency: EUR. *Firm.*
- **National Grid plc — United Kingdom (LSE: NG.; NYSE ADR: NGG; FTSE 100).** Owns and operates the electricity transmission network in England & Wales, historically the GB gas transmission system (sold as National Gas in 2023–24), and large US regulated networks (New England, New York). Overwhelmingly regulated T&D. **Crucially, it is GBP-denominated** — the clearest evidence that the German set was *not* strictly euro-only (contrast the E-Control euro-core restriction in §3.3). Currency: GBP. *Firm.*
- **Red Eléctrica / Redeia Corporación — Spain (Bolsa de Madrid: RED; IBEX 35).** The sole Spanish electricity TSO and system operator (~44,000 km of circuits). The parent was renamed Redeia in 2022; diversification (Hispasat satellites, Latin-American transmission) is comparatively small. Predominantly regulated electricity transmission. Currency: EUR. *Firm.*
- **REN — Redes Energéticas Nacionais — Portugal (Euronext Lisbon: RENE).** The rare dual operator: it runs both the Portuguese high-voltage electricity grid and the high-pressure gas network (plus gas storage/LNG access) under long concessions. Predominantly regulated transmission. Currency: EUR. *Firm.*
- **Snam S.p.A. — Italy (Euronext Milan: SRG; FTSE MIB).** One of Europe's largest gas infrastructure groups (~38,000 km of pipelines, plus storage and regasification), with international stakes (TAP, TAG, Interconnector UK, ADNOC Gas pipelines). Morningstar characterises it as "regulated revenue supports EBITDA." Predominantly regulated. Currency: EUR. *Firm.*
- **Terna S.p.A. — Italy (Euronext Milan: TRN; FTSE MIB).** The sole Italian electricity TSO (>74,000 km of high-voltage lines), almost entirely regulated transmission. Currency: EUR. *Firm.*

**Currency composition of the firm set: seven EUR plus one GBP (National Grid).** Of the eight, six are "pure" TSOs (Elia, Enagás, Red Eléctrica, REN, Snam, Terna), National Grid is a near-pure T&D operator denominated in sterling, and E.ON is the diversified outlier excluded from the narrow set.

They are **not** elevated to firm constituents here.

### 2.4 The screening funnel: from ~71 companies to the "pure" operators

The Gutachten proceeded in stages. Indexed text of the Frontier methodology (the 2016 report and its E-Control twin) supports a **four-stage funnel**:

1. **Starting universe ≈ 71 listed energy companies.** A broad universe of European/international listed energy and utility companies. (Frontier's description: "an extended sample of 71 companies," to which filter criteria — regulatory environment, regional distribution, business-field diversity, data availability and liquidity — were applied to identify the asset betas of pure grid operators.)
2. **Extended sample ≈ 23 companies** ("erweiterte Stichprobe") — the broader set whose values and metrics are tracked, "particularly containing companies with activities outside network operations."
3. **Narrower sample ≈ 12 companies** ("engere Stichprobe") — the empirical evaluation is "based on a narrower sample of 12 comparable companies."
4. **Pure network operators ≈ 9–11**, from which the asset-beta point estimate is formed — the continental TSO/network group with integrated utilities removed.

The 2016 report is generally described as retaining **9 pure operators** for asset-beta estimation; the 2021 update applied the same logic but, per indexed text of the 2021 Gutachten, the asset beta was taken as the **unweighted arithmetic mean of the risk factors of eleven comparison companies** as of 31 December 2020 (see §4.6). The discrepancy between "8 named in the FOI list," "9 pure operators in 2016" and "11 comparison companies in 2021" is best read as the difference between the *benchmark* set (8, including E.ON), the *2016 pure set* (9), and the *2021 asset-beta sample* (11, presumably the pure continental set plus a small number of gas-distribution/Italian names of the kind that populate the E-Control gas group). The exact identities of the 11 cannot be confirmed without the primary table; the "~71," "~23," "~12," "9" and "11" figures are reported-and-cross-referenced but not re-verified to the digit.

### 2.5 The "BIEGTRDT" E-Control peer group — corrected

The base note carried a single combined table tagged "BIEGTRDT" as the E-Control peer group. Deeper digging both **decodes the label** and supports several corrections:

- **"BIEGTRDT" is a Bloomberg index code**, not a company-initials acronym: it denotes Bloomberg Intelligence's **"BI Europe Gas Transmission & Distribution Top Competitive Peers"** index, which the E-Control gas-transmission report used as its core comparator group [6]. (The base note's working hypothesis that the letters encoded individual firms — I = Italgas, E = Enagás, T = Terna — is therefore wrong; no per-firm decode applies.)
- The E-Control **gas-transmission core group** sourced from this Bloomberg index is rendered as **Enagás (ES), Fluxys Belgium (BE), Italgas (IT), National Grid (UK), Redes Energéticas Nacionais/REN (PT) and Snam (IT)** — *six* names [6]. A parallel extraction of the broader E-Control gas peer set adds **A2A (IT) and Ascopiave (IT)** to reach eight [7]; the six-name core versus eight-name wider set could not be fully disambiguated, so treat the membership as six firmly (the BI index core) with A2A/Ascopiave as a probable wider-sample extension.
- Critically, **Terna and Elia are not in the E-Control gas group** — they belong to E-Control's *electricity* peer set. The base note's combined table therefore conflated the gas and electricity comparator sets (it placed Terna and A2A in the same "BIEGTRDT" list). In reality the German combined set and the E-Control *gas* set **overlap but are not identical**: common to both are Enagás, National Grid, REN and Snam; the German set adds the electricity TSOs (Elia, Red Eléctrica, Terna) and names E.ON before excluding it, while the E-Control gas set instead adds gas-distribution/Italian names (Italgas, Fluxys, and probably A2A/Ascopiave).

The practical upshot is that **Italgas and Fluxys are E-Control-confirmed but German-unconfirmed**, and the E-Control gas group is *not* a clean stand-in for the German combined group.

### 2.6 External validation from the UK

A strong independent cross-check that this specific continental set is *the* recognised European "pure network operator" universe for cost-of-equity work: for its **RIIO-T3** price control (final determinations December 2025), Ofgem provisionally **expanded its beta-comparator sample by adding five continental European regulated networks — Enagás, Redeia (Red Eléctrica), Italgas, Snam and Terna** — and its adviser **Oxera** concluded that their "business mixes and regulatory regimes … are sufficiently similar to GB energy networks" to be used in estimating the asset beta. Four of these (Enagás, Snam, Terna, Red Eléctrica) are in the German firm set; the fifth (Italgas) is the E-Control gas name. That two independent European regulators converge on the same comparator pool materially strengthens confidence in the *universe*, even though it does not pin the German *final sample*.

## 3. Selection criteria in depth

The selection criteria can be reconstructed from the Gutachten (via indexed text), the NERA account for BDEW, and the E-Control sister reports. The *concepts* are well supported; the *exact thresholds and wording* are, with one or two exceptions, corroborated rather than read verbatim, and are flagged accordingly.

### 3.1 Network-revenue purity (the contested >75 % test)

The decisive filter is that a comparator must earn a high share of revenue from **regulated network operation** ("reine Netzbetreiber"). Deeper digging upgrades this from the base note's NERA-attributed claim to a **verified, revenue-based threshold**: the OLG Düsseldorf (3 Kart 311/21) restates the definition as a comparison group of foreign listed pure network operators "where the network business comprises **more than 75 % of total business activity**," and the Frontier/E-Control methodology defines *reine Netzbetreiber* as companies where "der **Umsatzanteil des Netzgeschäfts** an dem Umsatz der gesamten unternehmerischen Aktivität zumindest 75 % beträgt" — i.e. the **share of network business in total revenue (Umsatz) is at least 75 %** [11][16][6]. The threshold is therefore **revenue-based, not EBIT-based**, and it is the central filtering criterion. (Whether NERA's BDEW report uses identical phrasing could not be re-confirmed verbatim, the two NERA PDFs being unreachable; but the 75 %-of-revenue test itself is now corroborated from the court and the Frontier/E-Control methodology.)

The mechanism by which diversified groups are excluded is documented in indexed text of the Randl/Zechner methodology, which states explicitly that **E.ON was not selected as a comparison company because it did not meet the requirement of a network-business revenue share greater than 75 %** [6]. The same logic excludes the other German diversified groups: comparison companies "would be particularly **RWE, E.ON and MVV**," but their values would need to be corrected for the risk premium attaching to **non-network business, which cannot be sufficiently done** on available data [9]. This is exactly why **E.ON, RWE and MVV are screened out** of the pure asset-beta set despite being large network owners. For E.ON specifically, the revenue-versus-EBIT split is the concrete reason a *revenue*-based purity test fails even though networks are E.ON's largest *earnings* driver (see §2.2). Of the extended 71-company sample, **eleven companies generating significant non-network revenue or earnings were excluded** from the narrow sample (see §4.6).

### 3.2 Regulatory and geographic comparability

Preference was given to companies operating under **European incentive-regulation regimes** comparable to the German Anreizregulierung — indexed text of the Randl/Zechner methodology states that "European companies were selected … because the regulatory system in Europe is best comparable to Germany" [6]. The geographic universe for the *peer/beta* set was Europe (the court language is "foreign listed pure network operators"), even though the CAPM *market index* was global (§4.1).

The statutory hook is **§ 7 Abs. 5 StromNEV** (mirrored in GasNEV), which directs that the surcharge for *netzbetriebsspezifische unternehmerische Wagnisse* be set, "in particular," taking account of: **Nr. 1** — conditions on national and international capital markets and the market valuation of network operators there; **Nr. 2** — the **average return on equity of operators of electricity/gas supply networks on foreign markets** (the explicit hook for the foreign-comparator peer group); and **Nr. 3** — observed and quantifiable entrepreneurial risks [25]. The courts read Nr. 2 as satisfied by representing foreign operators' returns *through the beta factor* of the foreign-pure-operator comparison group, rather than requiring a separate multi-jurisdiction quantification (see §5.3). (Authoritative text: gesetze-im-internet.de/stromnev/__7.html. The subsection numbering is genuinely confusing and was amended over time — sister sources variously cite § 7 Abs. 4, 5 and 6 — so the precise paragraph reference should still be cited with care; the substantive three-part content is now corroborated.)

### 3.3 Currency / market homogeneity — the euro-home-currency nuance

This is the most analytically important nuance in the selection criteria, and the evidence is genuinely two-sided — more so than the base note implied:

- The **euro-home-currency rationale is verified** in the E-Control reports. Indexed text states that "**six European companies** were selected as comparison companies … since the regulatory system in Europe is most comparable with Germany and the companies operate in the euro area, **with the euro as their home currency**. These six European companies represent the best comparable peer group, whereas international comparison companies are less comparable partly due to the differing currency" [6]. This is the "bestmögliche Vergleichsgruppe" framing the base note cites, and it is explicitly tied to the euro and to the rejection of non-euro comparators "due to the differing currency."
- **But the same E-Control gas-transmission report's core group (the BIEGTRDT/BI index, §2.5) demonstrably contains National Grid, a GBP name** [6]. So the euro-home-currency statement functions, at least in the gas-transmission report, as a *stated preference and rationale* rather than a *hard exclusion* — UK names were not categorically removed. A blanket claim that "the E-Control core set excludes all GBP/UK names" is therefore **not safe** for the gas-transmission report and is corrected here.
- The **German energy report likewise did not apply a strict euro-only filter.** The decisive evidence is that **National Grid (GBP) is in the German FOI benchmark set** [13], and the German-context comparator universe discussed by NERA is rendered with National Grid (and even Canada's TC Energy) present. The German report did, however, build in a "CAPM adaptation for the increasing integration of euro-area capital markets" — a euro-orientation that operates as a modelling adjustment rather than a currency exclusion.

The net picture is subtler than "Austria euro-only, Germany not": **both** the German and the E-Control gas reports admitted GBP National Grid, while **emphasising** a euro-home-currency preference and a euro-integration CAPM adjustment. Whether National Grid survived into the *narrowest* German asset-beta average of eleven, or the E-Control euro-only "six companies = best peer group" statement refers to a *different* (electricity) report or a narrower euro sub-group, cannot be resolved without the primary tables — and the apparent tension between the euro rationale and National Grid's retention is precisely the kind of consistency point the operators' experts pressed.

### 3.4 Stock liquidity

Only companies with **sufficient trading liquidity** for undistorted price formation were retained — a standard requirement, since illiquid stocks generate stale-price-biased, noisy betas. No specific liquidity threshold (free-float minimum, bid-ask cap, turnover floor or market-cap floor) could be recovered; the criterion is supported in concept only.

### 3.5 Length and quality of price history

Companies needed an **adequate continuous listed price history** to support beta estimation over multi-year windows (up to five years; §4.2). The requirement follows directly from the estimation design — five-year betas need at least five years of clean data — but no specific minimum (e.g. "at least 60 observations") could be recovered; supported in concept.

### 3.6 Exclusion of generation/supply-heavy businesses

The corollary of §3.1: companies with substantial generation, supply, retail or trading arms were excluded because those activities carry competitive, commodity-price-exposed risk distinct from regulated network operation. The named excluded diversified groups (E.ON, RWE, MVV) are precisely the firms with large generation/supply businesses.

### 3.7 The operators' critique of the criteria

The operators (via BDEW) and their experts (NERA, ValueTrust, Oxera, Hachmeister/Pedell) attacked the peer-group construction as **too narrow and downward-biased**: reducing ~71 candidates to a handful produces a small sample with high estimation error; a deliberately "pure," heavily-regulated peer group has lower systematic risk and hence a lower beta and a lower equity return; and the exclusion of diversified utilities whose network arms are large in absolute terms was contested (operators argued a sum-of-the-parts correction was feasible, contrary to the "cannot be sufficiently corrected" judgment). These criticisms are developed in §5.

## 4. Beta estimation methodology in depth

The CAPM-based beta machinery is the technical heart of the dispute. Several elements below were confirmed from indexed text of the Gutachten and the E-Control/railway reports; the remaining gaps — chiefly the debt-beta treatment and the exact 2021 German notional gearing — are flagged.

### 4.1 Reference market index (the market proxy)

**The German energy Gutachten used the FTSE All-World Index (and its sub-indices) as the CAPM market portfolio.** Indexed text of the report describes estimating beta from the ex-post returns of the peer group and "the market portfolio (FTSE All-World Index)," with raw betas then Vasicek- and Modigliani–Miller-adjusted. This is the concrete instantiation of the report's "global equity portfolio" premise (it assumes integrated international capital markets, so the representative investor holds a globally diversified portfolio). The **E-Control** reports use a market-cap-weighted **world portfolio ("Weltportfolio")** of industrialised countries; the **railway** work uses a world cap-weighted portfolio with STOXX indices featuring in the peer-beta step. The three report families thus share one philosophy; the German energy report specifically names FTSE All-World.

### 4.2 Estimation windows and return frequency

Raw betas were estimated over **multiple windows drawn from the {1-year, 3-year, 5-year} set** (the railway Methodenbericht's window-selection discussion explicitly weighs one-, three- and five-year periods). On **return frequency**, the sources diverge by sector and this should be flagged:

- The **railway** application is described as using **daily returns over a 3-year window** (with Vasicek adjustment) as the operative choice.
- The German **energy** report and the **E-Control** reports are described as using **weekly returns** — consistent with the authors' "2–3 year weekly plus longer windows" house style.

The daily-versus-weekly split between the railway and energy/Austrian work is a genuine discrepancy that could not be fully reconciled from the firewalled originals; the exact window length and frequency pairing for the German energy report should be confirmed directly. The peer-reviewed literature on exactly this regulatory setting (Gehrke et al., on beta estimation in European network regulation) supports the design choices: weekly/daily betas outperform monthly, three- and especially five-year betas are far more stable than one-year, and shrinkage adjustments toward 1.0 tend to *raise* low regulated-network betas — which is why the choice and strength of adjustment were contested.

### 4.3 Raw versus adjusted beta — Vasicek

Raw betas ("Rohbetas") were adjusted using the **Vasicek (1973) adjustment** — a Bayesian shrinkage that pulls each stock's raw regression beta toward a prior (the cross-sectional/peer mean), weighting the raw estimate and the prior inversely by their variances, so that a noisily-estimated beta is shrunk more strongly. Vasicek is confirmed as the standard across all three report families; **Blume** (the mechanical "two-thirds raw plus one-third of 1.0" rule) is named as the alternative but not adopted. The exact Vasicek prior (peer mean, sector prior, or 1.0) and the shrinkage weights could not be read and are unverified. Because the regulated-network betas sit *below* 1.0, shrinkage toward a higher prior tends to *raise* the estimate — so how strongly to shrink was itself a point of contention.

### 4.4 De-levering / re-levering — Modigliani–Miller, and the debt-beta question

Equity (levered) betas were unlevered to **asset (unlevered) betas company-by-company** using each peer's gearing, then re-levered to the regulatory notional capital structure, under the **Modigliani–Miller** framework. The MM label (rather than a Hamada tax-adjusted form) is consistent across the German, E-Control and railway descriptions; whether the specific MM variant carries a tax term or follows the tax-free Harris–Pringle form could not be confirmed.

The **debt beta** is the single most important unresolved technical point, and here this deep-dive corrects the base note:

- The base note assumed a **non-zero debt beta** for the German report.
- However, the methodologically identical **E-Control reports explicitly apply Modigliani–Miller de-/re-levering *without* a debt beta — "ohne Fremdkapitalbeta," i.e. debt beta = 0**, on a 40 % equity basis.
- Repeated targeted searches for a non-zero debt-beta *value* in the German energy Gutachten returned nothing. Given the identical-method E-Control practice, there is a real possibility the German report likewise unlevers/re-levers with **debt beta ≈ 0**.

**The debt-beta treatment for the German energy report is therefore unverified and should be checked directly** (search the de-/re-levering section of the Gutachten for "Fremdkapitalbeta," "Verschuldungsgrad," "Eigenkapitalquote"). A non-zero debt beta would raise the implied asset beta for a given equity beta and gearing; a zero debt beta is the more conservative reading and matches the Austrian twin.

### 4.5 Notional gearing / capital structure

- **E-Control: 40 % equity / 60 % debt** (nominal, after-tax) — well documented.
- **German fifth period (NEST reform): 40 % equity / 60 % debt** — well documented; the move to a standardised WACC adopts this notional structure.
- **German fourth-period energy report: unverified.** The fourth-period German approach worked through the regulatory equity-ratio cap (the long-standing "EK I" share of ~40 % under StromNEV/GasNEV, with equity above the cap remunerated at the debt rate). The re-levering target for the beta would be tied to this regulatory equity share, but the exact gearing used in the 2021 beta re-levering could not be read. The arithmetic is at least consistent: re-levering an asset beta of ~0.38 to an equity beta of 0.81 implies a substantial debt share, broadly in line with a ~40 % equity / ~60 % debt notional structure (especially with debt beta ≈ 0). This is an inference, not a verified figure.

### 4.6 Resulting betas — reconciling the 0.81

- **Re-levered (levered) equity beta = 0.81.** This is the "Risikofaktor" the OLG Düsseldorf cites, used as MRP 3.70 % × 0.81 ≈ 3.0 % (before the uplift). It is the best-verified number in the chapter, recurring across the OLG decisions and the Frontier summary [11][16].
- **Asset (unlevered) beta ≈ 0.34–0.42** (central value ≈ 0.38), taken as the **unweighted arithmetic mean of the risk factors of eleven comparison companies as of 31 December 2020**, unlevered via Modigliani–Miller (per indexed text of the Gutachten). The gap from ~0.38 to 0.81 is produced by re-levering at the high notional debt share.
- **Raw beta (Rohbeta):** no headline numeric value could be recovered — unverified.
- **E-Control unlevered beta ≈ 0.325** — a close cross-check: an asset beta of ~0.32–0.34 re-levered at 40/60 (D/E = 1.5) with a small or zero debt beta yields an equity beta in the high-0.7s to ~0.8 range, consistent with 0.81. This corroborates the interpretation that 0.81 is the re-levered equity beta and the asset beta is ~0.32–0.42.

A note on the headline 3.39 % Wagniszuschlag (for §5): the surcharge is **not** simply 3.70 % × 0.81 ≈ 3.0 %. BNetzA added approximately **0.395 percentage points** to reach ≈ 3.39 %, a partial correction for the fact that the risk-free base rate had fallen much faster (from 2.49 % to 0.74 %) than the bond-relative MRP, addressing the "convenience-yield"/quality-of-debt gap on German sovereign yields. The MRP of 3.70 % itself is described as a midpoint of an arithmetic (~4.30 %) and geometric (~3.10 %) mean from the DMS dataset in the Credit Suisse *Global Investment Returns Yearbook 2021* (1900–2020).

## 5. From peer group/beta to the Wagniszuschlag, and the contested peer-group choice

### 5.1 The numbers

The peer-group beta fed directly into the allowed return:

- **Beta (risk factor) = 0.81** × **MRP 3.70 % ≈ 3.0 %** (the core CAPM equity risk premium).
- **+ ≈ 0.395 percentage points** discretionary uplift → **Wagniszuschlag ≈ 3.39 %** [16].
- **+ risk-free base rate 0.74 %** (ten-year trailing average of the Umlaufsrendite/Bundesbank yields, down from 2.49 % in the third period) [3].
- Grossed up by a tax factor of ≈ 1.226, this produced the determined pre-tax equity rates: **5.07 % for new assets** and **3.51 % for existing assets** [3][9].

The **nature of the 0.395 pp uplift is now better pinned down**. It is not a discretionary political add-on but a **methodological-consistency correction**: BNetzA recognised an inconsistency between the risk-free base rate defined by § 7 Abs. 4 StromNEV/GasNEV (the German ten-year Umlaufsrendite average) and the bond proxy used as the risk-free estimator *inside* the DMS dataset that underpins the MRP; left uncorrected, the two together would *understate* the surcharge. After the consultation — and, on the indexed reading of the OLG decisions, explicitly prompted by a **second Oxera report** submitted by the operators — BNetzA raised the surcharge by 0.395 pp over its July 2021 draft [16][18]. The BGH later found this uplift plausible and adequately reasoned.

On the **draft-versus-final** sequence: BNetzA's July 2021 **draft** set **4.59 % (new) / 3.03 % (existing)**, pre-tax, with a Wagniszuschlag of ≈ 3.0 %; the **final** October 2021 determination set **5.07 % (new) / 3.51 % (existing)**, pre-tax, with the Wagniszuschlag at 3.39 % — the 0.395 pp uplift flowing through the ~1.226 tax gross-up to lift both rates [1][3][9].

This **resolves the existing-asset discrepancy** flagged in the base note and report 03 (which variously cite 3.51 % uniform and 3.03 % for gas): the **3.03 % is the July 2021 draft figure, not a final gas-specific rate**. The final existing-asset rate is **3.51 %, applied uniformly to electricity and gas** — BK4-21-0055 and BK4-21-0056 are "im Wesentlichen gleichlautend," and a *gas* case (OLG 3 Kart 689/21) itself states 3.51 % for existing (Alt-)assets, direct evidence that gas was not given a separate 3.03 % [3][11]. There is no electricity-versus-gas split in the final figures. (One arithmetical caution: 5.07 % derives as after-tax new-asset 4.13 % × 1.226, not as 3.51 % × 1.22; 3.51 % is the pre-tax existing-asset rate.)

### 5.2 The contested peer-group choice — the operators' experts' alternatives

Peer-group composition and beta were the central battleground. The operators argued the BNetzA/Frontier-Zechner-Randl peer group was **too narrow and downward-biased**; the consumer side argued the opposite. The principal counter-Gutachten:

- **NERA Economic Consulting (for BDEW), *Vergleich internationaler Eigenkapitalzinssätze*, 11 June 2021** [5]. A benchmarking attack rather than a re-estimation of beta: across **17 comparison countries** NERA put the **average allowed equity rate at ≈ 6.05 % after tax**, versus Germany's third-period **5.64 % after tax** — i.e. Germany ~**1.4–1.5 percentage points below** the international average, with the gap widening to >2 pp once the fourth-period cut took effect; updates lifted the international average toward ~6.13 %/7.13 %. The best-attested after-tax bandwidth across the countries is **≈ 3.74 % (Portugal, lowest) to 8.70 % (Sweden, highest)**; the "4.30 %–7.50 %" range and the "implicit/realised cost of equity 6.82–7.04 %" cited in the base note could not be reproduced and should be treated as uncertain or as a different cut of the same data. A companion NERA report, *Eigenkapitalzinssatz — internationaler Vergleich der Festlegungen* [14], made the more substantive point that peer regulators *explicitly or implicitly account for the inverse relationship between the risk-free rate and the MRP* (via alternative methods or ad-hoc uplifts) — which BNetzA did only minimally through the 0.395 pp. NERA argued that a comparable German *nominal* rate of order ~11.6 % would be needed to keep network investment internationally competitive.
- **ValueTrust (for BDEW), *Gutachtliche Stellungnahme zur kapitalmarktkonformen Ermittlung CAPM-basierter Eigenkapitalkosten*, 9 July 2021** [17]. Argued that essential scientific standards — **methodological pluralism, consistency of input parameters, and plausibility checks** — were insufficiently observed, and called for forward-looking elements and cross-checks beyond a single historical-DMS CAPM. This "Methodenpluralismus" line became the operators' signature argument.
- **Oxera (for Netze BW/EnBW), *Bestimmung des Wagniszuschlags — Stellungnahme zum Gutachten von Frontier Economics*, 19 August 2021** [18]. Oxera's central attack was on the use of the **DMS *world* market risk premium**: it argued a world MRP is inappropriately low for setting a German/European regulated cost of equity, and that the regulator inconsistently mixed a *national* risk-free rate with a *world* MRP. Notably, Oxera's critique provoked a **direct rebuttal from Dimson, Marsh and Staunton themselves** (24 August 2021), defending how their dataset should be used in a regulatory context — a rare instance of the dataset's authors entering the fray.
- **Hachmeister/Pedell (Prof. Dr. Dirk Hachmeister, Universität Hohenheim; Prof. Dr. Burkhard Pedell, Universität Stuttgart), counter-Gutachten for BDEW, 20 August 2021** (a sibling opinion was commissioned by 50Hertz; an updated version dated 17 June 2024 addresses the fifth-period/NEST process) [10]. The senior-academic counterweight to Zechner/Randl, identifying the same methodological deficits as ValueTrust — lack of methodological pluralism, inconsistent inputs, inadequate plausibility checks, and insufficient forward-looking elements.
- **vzbv and Prof. Thomas Wein (Leuphana Universität Lüneburg), Stellungnahme, 25 August 2021** [19]. The consumer pole, arguing the rates were **too high**: using 2021 data would imply a risk-free rate of only **0.47 %**, the Wagniszuschlag should be cut to **2.36 %**, and the resulting existing-asset rate of **3.47 %** would save electricity customers around **€1.1 billion**. (For LichtBlick, Prof. Wein advanced the same "too high" direction.) The existence of credible attacks from *both* sides — operators saying the rate was too low, consumers saying too high — is exactly what the courts later treated as evidence that BNetzA's middle position was defensible.

### 5.3 How the courts resolved the peer-group dispute

It is important to separate the *peer-group/beta* dispute from the *MRP/DMS* dispute that dominated the litigation:

- The **OLG Düsseldorf** (lead and parallel decisions of **30 August 2023** — 3 Kart 311/21, 498/21, 544/21, 689/21, 757/21, 775/21, 878/21, 908/21) **annulled** the determinations, but on two grounds, **neither of which was the peer group**: (i) the substantive defect was the **market risk premium** — BNetzA failed to *plausibilise* the MRP derived solely from the DMS historical series, in particular the risk that the arithmetic mean overstates the premium for low-volatility regulated firms; and (ii) a **procedural defect** — alleged one-sided partisanship of the advisory board (*Beirat*) in favour of higher network returns. On the peer-group/beta specifically, the senate **recited and did not condemn** the methodology: beta was derived "anhand einer Vergleichsgruppe ausländischer börsennotierter reiner Netzbetreiber," the comparison group was chosen by reference to the most current available decisions, and estimation uncertainty was handled via the **Vasicek** method [11][12][16]. In other words, the peer-group methodology survived OLG scrutiny; it was the MRP (and a procedural point) that failed at first instance.
- The **BGH** then reversed the OLG and **upheld BNetzA** across a series of decisions — electricity on 17 December 2024 (EnVR 79/23 and related, holding the rate was set "ohne Rechtsfehler") and gas on 25 February 2025 (EnVR 83/23, 86/23, 90/23, 93/23), with a further confirmatory decision (EnVR 82/23, ECLI:DE:BGH:2025:090925BENVR82.23.0) on **9 September 2025** [20]. It set a deferential standard: where StromNEV/GasNEV contain no more specific rule, BNetzA enjoys a **Beurteilungsspielraum**, and its choice among recognised methods stands unless the chosen approach is "grundlegend ungeeignet" (fundamentally unsuitable) or another is "deutlich überlegen" (clearly superior). **The key holding on the peer group**: the BGH found that the § 7 Abs. 5 Nr. 2 requirement to consider foreign operators' average equity returns is **satisfied through the peer-group/beta methodology itself** — "foreseeable industry-specific risks from the energy transition are already taken into account through the inclusion of listed (gas) network operators as comparison companies in the determination of the beta factor," so no separate, free-standing quantification of foreign allowed returns was required [20]. On that basis BNetzA's beta derivation, and the 0.395 pp uplift, were lawful and required no further investigation. (Note the date correction relative to some secondary summaries: EnVR 82/23 is a 9 September 2025 decision, not part of the 25 February 2025 gas cohort.)

Notably, in neither the OLG nor the BGH decisions do the recoverable passages **enumerate the specific peer companies**; the courts refer to the peer group *generically* by category. The courts therefore confirm the *method*, not the *names* — which is precisely why the FragDenStaat disclosure remains the only firm source for the constituents.

### 5.4 Synthesis

The verifiable record establishes a **combined European energy-network peer group** anchored on Italian, Spanish, Portuguese and Belgian pure TSOs plus (for benchmarking) UK National Grid — Snam, Terna, Enagás, Red Eléctrica/Redeia, REN, Elia and National Grid, with E.ON named but screened out — drawn from an extended universe of ~71 companies through a four-stage funnel (~71 → ~23 → ~12 → ~9–11). Betas were estimated against the FTSE All-World Index, Vasicek-adjusted, and Modigliani–Miller de-/re-levered to a notional regulatory gearing, producing an asset beta of ≈ 0.34–0.42 and a re-levered equity beta of **0.81**, which drove a Wagniszuschlag of ≈ 3.39 % and, with a 0.74 % base rate, pre-tax equity rates of 5.07 % (new) / 3.51 % (existing). The exact membership of the eleven-company asset-beta sample, the debt-beta treatment, and the precise fourth-period notional gearing remain to be confirmed against the primary Gutachten table in BK4-21-0055.

The most explicit statement of the windows (1/3/5 yr; operative daily/3-yr), Vasicek and MM machinery. *Indexed text only.* [2][15]

### 6.2 Counter-Gutachten (the operators' and consumers' side)

8. **NERA (for BDEW), *Vergleich internationaler Eigenkapitalzinssätze*, 11 June 2021** — international benchmarking; 17-country after-tax average ≈ 6.05 % versus Germany's third-period 5.64 %, i.e. Germany ~1.4–1.5 pp below (range across decisions ≈ 3.74–8.70 % after tax). Exact date unverified (2021; a 2020 predecessor exists). *Indexed text only.* [14]
10. **ValueTrust (for BDEW), *Gutachtliche Stellungnahme zur kapitalmarktkonformen Ermittlung CAPM-basierter Eigenkapitalkosten*, 9 July 2021** — "methodological pluralism / consistency of inputs / plausibility checks" critique. Lead author unverified. *Indexed text only.* [17]
11. **Oxera (for Netze BW), *Bestimmung des Wagniszuschlags — Stellungnahme zum Gutachten von Frontier Economics*, 19 August 2021** — central attack on the DMS *world* MRP and the national-RFR/world-MRP inconsistency; provoked a direct rebuttal from Dimson, Marsh and Staunton (24 Aug 2021). *Indexed text only.* [18]

### 6.3 Academic and background underpinnings

14. **Dimson, Marsh & Staunton, *Credit Suisse Global Investment Returns Yearbook 2021*** (21st edition; authors Elroy Dimson, Cambridge Judge; Paul Marsh and Mike Staunton, London Business School; publisher Credit Suisse Research Institute — the Yearbook moved to UBS from the 2023 edition). ### 6.4 What the courts add to the literature

The OLG Düsseldorf decisions of 30 August 2023 and the BGH decisions of 17 December 2024 / 25 February 2025 / 9 September 2025 are, paradoxically, among the richest *accessible* descriptions of the Gutachten's internals — they recite the beta (0.81), the MRP (3.70 %), the Wagniszuschlag (3.39 %), the base rate (0.74 %) and the +0.395 pp uplift in prose [11][12][16][20]. But they describe the peer group only by category ("foreign listed pure network operators") and do not enumerate the constituents — which is why the FragDenStaat disclosure remains indispensable.

## 7. Verification posture and open items

This report's central claim — the identity of the peer group — rests on a single freedom-of-information disclosure that could not be re-opened verbatim in this research. The following items remain **unverified or uncertain** and should be confirmed against the primary Gutachten (BK4-21-0055/0056) and its annexes before being relied upon:

- The **exact membership of the eleven-company asset-beta sample** for 2021 (the eight FOI names are firm for the benchmark set; whether Italgas/Fluxys/A2A/Ascopiave or others filled out the sample to eleven is unconfirmed for Germany).
- The **debt-beta treatment** in the German report (the identical E-Control method uses **no debt beta**; the base note's "non-zero debt beta" could not be confirmed and may be wrong).
- The **fourth-period notional gearing** used in the German beta re-levering (likely ~40 % equity, by analogy to EK I and the NEST 40/60, but not read).
- The **return frequency** for the German energy beta (weekly per indexed text, but the railway twin uses daily — confirm directly).
- Whether the E-Control euro-only "**six companies = best peer group**" statement refers to the *electricity* report or a narrower euro sub-group, given that the gas-transmission core (BIEGTRDT) retained GBP National Grid.
- The exact **NERA after-tax range** (3.74–8.70 % is the better-attested figure; the base note's 4.30–7.50 % could not be reproduced) and NERA's "implicit/realised CoE 6.82–7.04 %" (unverified).
- The **specific 5.5 %/6 % fifth-period MRP** sometimes quoted (the verified range is 3.2–4.4 % vs long-term government bonds).

Items the deeper digging **resolved or upgraded** since the base note: (i) the **>75 % network-purity threshold is verified as revenue-based** (restated by the OLG and the Frontier/E-Control methodology), with E.ON's exclusion expressly tied to it; (ii) **"BIEGTRDT" is decoded** as Bloomberg's "BI Europe Gas Transmission & Distribution Top Competitive Peers" index, and the E-Control gas/electricity table is corrected (Terna and Elia are *not* in the gas group); (iii) the **FTSE All-World** market index, the **~71→23→12→9–11 funnel**, and the **0.34–0.42 asset beta / 0.81 equity beta** reconciliation are verified; (iv) the **existing-asset rate is confirmed at 3.51 % uniform** (3.03 % was the July 2021 draft); (v) the **+0.395 pp uplift** is identified as an rf/MRP consistency correction prompted by a second Oxera report; and (vi) a **likely error in the base note's non-zero debt-beta assumption** is flagged. No deeper digging produced a firmly **new** German constituent or **refuted** one from the base note's eight-company list.
