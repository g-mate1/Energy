# Peer Groups in the Randl/Zechner Expert Reports for German Gas and Electricity Network Returns

*This report was compiled from publicly available sources as of June 2026. Several primary documents — in particular the Frontier/Zechner/Randl Gutachten hosted on bundesnetzagentur.de — could not be retrieved verbatim because the agency's document server blocks automated extraction; the findings below were therefore triangulated from official press releases, court decisions (OLG Düsseldorf, BGH), freedom-of-information disclosures, consultant statements, and the authors' methodologically identical reports for the Austrian regulator E-Control. Where the exact membership of the narrowest peer set could not be read directly from the primary Gutachten, this is flagged explicitly. Readers should consult the original Gutachten (BNetzA file BK4-21-0055/0056) to confirm exact peer-group membership and beta figures.*

## 1. Authors and Reports

Otto Randl and Josef Zechner are professors of finance at WU Vienna (Vienna University of Economics and Business). Zechner holds the chair of finance and investments; Randl is a member of the Institute for Finance, Banking and Insurance. The two are among the most frequently commissioned academic experts on regulatory cost of capital in the German-speaking area, working for both the German Bundesnetzagentur (BNetzA) and the Austrian regulator E-Control, and (with Frontier Economics and IGES) for BNetzA's railway-infrastructure cost-of-capital reviews [1][2][6].

For the German energy networks, Randl and Zechner co-authored — together with **Frontier Economics Ltd.** — the **"Wissenschaftliches Gutachten zur Ermittlung der Zuschläge für unternehmerische Wagnisse von Strom- und Gasnetzbetreibern"** ("Scientific Expert Report on Determining the Surcharges for Entrepreneurial Risks of Electricity and Gas Network Operators"), published by BNetzA in **July 2021** [1][3][9]. The report supports the determination of the *Wagniszuschlag* (risk premium on equity) under § 7 Abs. 5 StromNEV/GasNEV. A companion report on the market risk premium was authored by Professors **Stehle and Betzer** [3].

These reports underpinned BNetzA's *Eigenkapitalzinssatz* decisions for the **4th regulatory period** — electricity decision **BK4-21-0055** and gas decision **BK4-21-0056**, both dated **12 October 2021** [3][9][10]. The framework was carried into the January 2024 redetermination of the return on new assets [4]. The methodology is essentially identical to the Frontier/Zechner/Randl approach first used for the 3rd period in 2016, and to Randl/Zechner's E-Control WACC reports for Austrian gas-transmission and electricity-transmission operators (2019, 2022, 2023, 2024) [6][7][8].

## 2. Peer-Group Companies Used to Estimate Beta

BNetzA's risk factor (beta) was, in the words of the OLG Düsseldorf, derived "based on a comparison group of foreign, stock-listed *pure* network operators" ("anhand einer Vergleichsgruppe ausländischer börsennotierter reiner Netzbetreiber") [11][12]. The Gutachten did **not** publish separate gas-only and electricity-only peer groups; consistent with the legislator's requirement of a uniform equity rate for both sectors, a **single combined European energy-network peer group** was used [3][11].

The most authoritative attribution of the actual constituents comes from a Bundesnetzagentur freedom-of-information disclosure (via FragDenStaat) of the Frontier/Zechner/Randl Gutachten, which lists the international benchmarking/peer companies as: **E.ON, Elia (Elia System Operator), Enagás, National Grid, Red Eléctrica, Redes Energéticas Nacionais (REN), Snam, and Terna** [13]. This set is corroborated by NERA's international comparison for the BDEW, which describes a peer group of comparable European network operators [5][14].

The methodology proceeded in two stages [3][9]:
- an **extended sample of ~71 listed energy companies** was screened;
- filters (regulatory environment, geographic diversification, business-field diversification, network-revenue purity, liquidity) reduced this to a small set of **"pure" electricity and gas network operators** used for the asset-beta calculation. Frontier's own description of the predecessor 2016 report identifies **9 pure network operators** retained for asset-beta estimation from an extended sample of 71 [9]; the 2021 update applied the same logic.

The constituent table below distinguishes companies that are firmly attributable to the German Gutachten's peer/benchmark set from those that are documented constituents of Randl/Zechner's **methodologically identical E-Control reports** (the "BIEGTRDT" peer group), which are useful corroboration but were calibrated to Austrian, not German, operators.

## 3. Selection Criteria

The reported and reconstructed selection criteria were [3][5][9]:

1. **Network-revenue purity.** Comparable companies should earn a high share of revenue from regulated network operation. NERA's account of the methodology records a threshold of **>75% of revenue from the network business**; **E.ON failed this test** and was excluded from the asset-beta set, as values for diversified groups (E.ON, RWE, MVV) "cannot be sufficiently corrected" for the risk of non-network activities [5][9].
2. **Regulatory and geographic comparability.** Preference for companies operating under European incentive-regulation regimes most comparable to Germany [5][9].
3. **Currency / market homogeneity.** In the sister E-Control reports the *core* peer group is restricted to **Eurozone operators with the euro as home currency**, "the best possible comparable peer group," to avoid currency-mismatch distortions [5][7]. (BNetzA's own benchmark set additionally referenced an FTSE All-World market index, see §4.)
4. **Stock liquidity.** Only companies with sufficient trading liquidity for undistorted price formation were retained [9].
5. **Length and quality of price history.** Adequate continuous listed history is required to estimate beta over multi-year windows [9].
6. **Exclusion of generation/supply-heavy businesses** (the corollary of criterion 1).

## 4. Beta Estimation Methodology

The CAPM-based methodology can be reconstructed as follows [3][5][9][11][12]:

- **Reference market index.** A broad **global equity index (FTSE All-World)** was used for the German energy determination as the market proxy [5]. (The methodologically related E-Control reports use a world equity portfolio; the German railway reports use STOXX indices [2][6].)
- **Return frequency and estimation windows.** Returns were measured at relatively high frequency (**daily/weekly**), with raw betas estimated over **multiple overlapping windows** — typically **1-year, 3-year and 5-year** horizons — to reflect uncertainty about the relevant period for forming investor expectations [7][2]. The authors' standard practice combines a **2–3 year weekly** estimation with longer windows [15].
- **Raw vs adjusted beta.** Raw betas were adjusted using the **Vasicek adjustment**, which shrinks estimates toward a prior to account for estimation risk; the authors discuss Blume-type adjustment as the alternative reference but adopt Vasicek as standard in the regulatory context [7][15].
- **De-levering / re-levering.** Equity (levered) betas were unlevered to **asset betas** company-by-company using each peer's gearing, then re-levered to the regulatory capital structure. The framework follows **Modigliani–Miller / Hamada** with a non-zero **debt beta** (the parallel E-Control/Frontier work applies an MM transformation with an explicit debt beta and notional gearing) [7][15].
- **Capital structure.** A notional regulatory gearing was applied in re-levering. (The precise German gearing assumption sits in the BK4-21-0055 decision and could not be read verbatim; BNetzA's regulatory equity share for the affected assets is the relevant anchor.)
- **Resulting figures.** The peer-group analysis produced a **risk factor (beta) of 0.81**, which, multiplied by a **market risk premium of 3.70%**, yielded a **Wagniszuschlag of ≈3.0%** [3][16][11]. With a risk-free base rate of **0.74%** (ten-year trailing average of Bundesbank yields), BNetzA set the pre-tax equity rate at **5.07% for new assets and 3.51% (electricity) / 3.03% (gas) for existing assets**; the network-specific risk surcharge in the decision was stated as **3.39%** [3][11][16].

## 5. From Peer Group to Wagniszuschlag, and the Dispute

The peer-group beta of 0.81 was the single largest driver of the 3% risk premium and hence of the allowed return. Because German regulation mandates a uniform equity rate across electricity and gas, the combined European network peer group fed directly into one beta and one *Wagniszuschlag* [3][11].

Peer-group composition and beta were the **central battleground** in the operators' challenges. The operators' experts argued the BNetzA/Frontier-Zechner-Randl peer group was **too narrow and downward-biased**:
- **NERA** (for BDEW) ran an international comparison concluding that German allowed equity (≈5–7% range) lagged European peers by ~1.5 percentage points after tax, and criticised the capital-structure adjustments and the exclusion of relevant comparators [5][14].
- **ValueTrust** (for BDEW) challenged the CAPM implementation and beta methodology [17].
- **Oxera** prepared "Bestimmung des Wagniszuschlags — Stellungnahme zum Gutachten von Frontier Economics" (Aug 2021) for operators (published e.g. by Netze BW/EnBW), disputing the MRP and beta derivation [18].
- **Hachmeister/Pedell** and **Prof. Wein** (the latter for LichtBlick, arguing the rate was *too high*) submitted further counter-Gutachten, illustrating that the peer-group/beta dispute cut both ways [10][19].

The courts ultimately sided with the regulator. The **OLG Düsseldorf** (lead decisions of **30 August 2023**, e.g. 3 Kart 878/21, 689/21, 311/21, 544/21) dismissed the operators' complaints, holding that BNetzA's beta derivation from a comparison group of foreign listed pure network operators, and its consideration of foreign regulators' equity rates under § 7 Abs. 5 Nr. 2 GasNEV, were not objectionable [11][12]. The **BGH** confirmed this in a series of decisions on **25 February 2025** (EnVR 82/23, 83/23, 86/23, 90/23, 93/23), finding that BNetzA's "thoroughly determined results" required no further investigation and that the *Wagniszuschlag* — including the increase over the July 2021 draft — was lawful [20].

In sum: the verifiable record establishes a **combined European energy-network peer group** anchored on Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Belgian and (for benchmarking) UK pure network operators — Snam, Terna, Enagás, Red Eléctrica, REN, Elia and National Grid, with E.ON screened out — estimated with daily/weekly returns over 1/3/5-year windows, Vasicek-adjusted, MM-unlevered/relevered with a debt beta, producing β ≈ 0.81 and a 3% risk premium. The exact membership of the narrowest asset-beta set, and the precise gearing and debt-beta inputs, remain to be confirmed against the primary Gutachten table in BK4-21-0055.
